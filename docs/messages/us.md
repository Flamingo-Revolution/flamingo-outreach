# Flamingo Protests in Albania — English awareness emails (United States)

A United States edition of the [Letters](index.md). Recipients here are US media, US institutions and Congress — not EU institutions or MEPs. Choose the version that fits your recipient, add a few words of your own, and send it in your name. After "Kind regards", add your name or your organisation's name.

=== "US Media"

    **Subject:** Albania’s student protests: request for US reporting and verification

    Dear editorial team,

    **How your newsroom can help**

    I am writing to ask your newsroom to investigate reports of police abuses during Albania’s student-led demonstrations, which began in Tirana on 13 October 2026 and include high school pupils. Please let me know whether your newsroom can pursue this story for US audiences, including Albanian-American communities. The figures, source links and further resources below provide a starting point.

    - Investigate the reports by speaking with students, families, lawyers and journalists, and checking the available footage and records.
    - Seek an on-record response from the Albanian authorities about the use of force and the treatment of minors in custody.
    - Cover the findings and follow up on whether complaints lead to independent investigations and accountability.

    **Why action is needed**

    The demonstrations follow more than 150 days of Flamingo protests, which began over development in the protected Vjosë–Nartë landscape and expanded to public services, accountability and the rule of law ([protest chronology](https://www.flamingorevolution.eu/protestat/)). These figures cover different periods and groups, overlap, and must not be added together. These are attributed reports and allegations requiring independent verification, not established legal findings. Key reported figures include:

    - **1,067 arrests; 388 criminal proceedings:** Reuters, citing Albanian authorities.
    - **310 injured through 19 October:** 191 police, 88 protesters and 31 bystanders (*El País*, citing the Health Ministry).
    - **214 detainees met:** earlier Ombudsman monitoring, cited by Res Publica, recorded visible signs of violence or mistreatment allegations for 26 people and reports of about 12 minors allegedly held in isolation.
    - **32 reported police-violence cases:** Res Publica’s annex covering 13–17 October; not final legal findings.
    - **124 evidence items reviewed:** lawyers’ letter to Amnesty International, including 31 new log entries for 18–20 October. The confidential log is not public.

    **What Members of Congress are doing**

    Two House members have publicly raised concerns about the protests. Both statements are reported secondhand by Albanian outlets; only Lawler's original post could be confirmed directly.

    - **Mike Lawler (R-NY):** Posted on X that he had heard from concerned Albanian-American constituents and called on the Albanian government to de-escalate tensions and end the use of force against peaceful protesters. [↗](https://x.com/RepMikeLawler/status/2113296315018854596)
    - **Ritchie Torres (D-NY):** Said he is "deeply concerned" about violence against protesters, affirmed Albanians' right to protest peacefully, and called on police to respect that right (reported by BalkanWeb, 22 October 2026). [↗](https://www.balkanweb.com/?p=3066587)

    No confirmed 2026 statement from a sitting US Senator or from the Helsinki Commission (CSCE) was found when this page was checked on 8 November 2026; the CSCE's only Albania press release on file dates to 2011.

    Kind regards,
